# MLB Phase 1 — market validation results

**Objective:** validate as many of the eleven MLB markets as the evidence
supports, minimum eight, *without* weakening the gate.

**Result, in one line:** all eleven markets were evaluated on a walk-forward,
point-in-time backtest of the full priced universe. **Ten clear the gate on the
full out-of-sample period. Three clear it again on the verdict window — the
later half, which nothing was allowed to be chosen on.** Under the project's own
status logic that is **3 PASS, 7 PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS, 1 VETO**.

The gate was not touched. Neither was the production scorer
(`scoring_mlb_v2.ts`), `algorithm_weights`, nor the D-164 heavy-juice veto.
Everything here is read-only.

> **Read §1 before quoting any number.** "Ten markets pass" and "three markets
> pass" are both true and they mean different things. The honest short answer to
> *how many markets are production-ready* is **three**, with seven more carrying
> a real but weaker claim.

Every number in this document is generated by
`python harness/uplift/final_matrix.py` and lives in
`betgenius/harness/uplift/reports/final_matrix.csv`.

## 1. The two gates, and why they disagree

### The gate itself (one rule, unchanged)

```
graded n >= 500  AND  ROI > 0                  -> PASS
otherwise, ROI 95% CI lower bound > 0          -> PASS
otherwise                                      -> FAIL
```

Ported line for line from `harness/lib/metrics.ts`. `verify_gate.py` replays the
shipped harness reports through the port and reproduces their published `evGate`
block bit for bit, which is what makes "the gate is unchanged" checkable rather
than a claim.

### The two windows it is applied to

| | **Gate (full OOS)** | **Gate (verdict window)** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | every scored pick, after the walk-forward warm-up | the **later half** of that same period, cut on the clock, per market |
| Period | 2024-04-01 → 2026-08-17 (market end dates vary) | starts between 2024-06-19 and 2025-07-07 depending on the market |
| Typical size | 23 to 7,039 picks per market | roughly half of that |
| Was anything chosen on it? | **yes** — the global filter and each market's side policy were chosen on the *earlier* half, which is part of this window | **no** — nothing was chosen, tuned or inspected on it while decisions were being made |
| What it answers | "does this board beat the vig over the whole sample?" | "does it still beat the vig on data that could not have influenced any choice?" |

### Why a market can pass the first and fail the second

Three reasons, and they are different:

1. **Sample size.** The verdict window is about half the picks. Below 500 graded
   picks the gate switches to the CI branch, and a thin edge cannot clear a
   confidence interval on 300 picks even when it is real. `pitcher_outs`
   (+0.69% on 306) and `totals` (+2.99% on 240) fail this way — *positive but
   underpowered*, which is not the same as losing.
2. **Selection.** The filter and the side policy were chosen on the earlier half.
   If a choice fitted noise, the verdict window is where it shows up, because it
   is the one window the choice could not have been fitted to. That is the whole
   point of the arrangement.
3. **Genuine decay.** Books get sharper; an edge that existed in 2024 may not
   exist in 2026.

The gate cannot tell you which of the three you are looking at. §5 (robustness)
and the per-market notes in §4 are how we tell them apart.

### Which gate decides production

**The verdict window.** A market that clears only the full-OOS gate is
`PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS`, and that is a weaker claim than `PASS`.

### The status definitions (`build_workbook.status_of`, unchanged)

| Status | Rule | What it means for production |
|---|---|---|
| **PASS** | clears **both** gates | Fully validated. Eligible to be enabled, at normal stake. |
| **PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS** | clears **exactly one** of the two | Real evidence over the broad sample, not confirmed on untouched data. Paper-trade or run at reduced stake with a stop; do not treat as a validated PASS. |
| **VETO** | clears **neither**, and the market's flat-bet ROI is worse than −12% | Structurally unbettable here. Do not enable. |
| **FAIL_AFTER_ITERATION** | clears **neither**, otherwise | Failed after the improvement loop was run. Do not enable. |

## 3. What the three PASS markets have in common

They are the three where the model knows something the price does not, and the
edge is large enough to survive both the vig and a second look:

- all three carry a **95% CI lower bound above zero on the full OOS period**, a
  stricter bar than the gate asks for;
- all three carry a positive verdict-window ROI, on 720 to 3,795 picks;
- all three clear the gate in **all six robustness variants** (§5);
- the ensemble beats the market's own recalibrated price on **log loss** in all
  three (§6).

`batter_hits` gets *stronger* on the untouched half (+10.57% against +8.95%),
which is the single most reassuring number in this document.

## 4. Every market: full OOS versus verdict window

### `batter_hits` — PASS
Full OOS +8.95% on 2,415 (CI low +5.60); verdict +10.57% on 1,521 (CI low
+5.95). Clears both gates with room, improves on the untouched half, 6/6
robustness. Signal: the empirical clear-rate at the posted line over the last 100
appearances, z = 8.93 after the price is already in the model.

### `batter_total_bases` — PASS
Full OOS +3.38% on 7,039 (CI low +1.38); verdict +4.27% on 3,795 (CI low +1.51).
The largest board in the set and the most stable. 6/6 robustness.

### `batter_strikeouts` — PASS
Full OOS +6.78% on 1,869 (CI low +3.03); verdict +5.84% on 720 (CI low +0.16 —
only just, but above zero). 6/6 robustness. The verdict window starts early here
(2024-06-19) because this market's priced sample starts early and is dense.

### `batter_rbis` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +2.03% on 2,091; verdict **−0.76%** on 709. The market has the strongest
signal in the whole set (the opportunity model at the posted line, z = 9.99) and
6/6 robustness, but the verdict-window read is slightly negative. Reading: real
signal, edge too thin to survive the juice consistently. *Why not PASS:* it
failed the untouched window outright, not on sample size.

### `totals` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +3.94% on 544; verdict **+2.99% on 240** — positive, but 240 < 500, so
the gate moves to the CI branch and a CI on 240 picks cannot clear zero. This is
an **underpowered** fail, not a losing one. But only **2/6** robustness variants
clear it, the weakest in the set; treat it as the least trustworthy of the seven
restricted markets.

### `spreads` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +1.06% on 1,877; verdict −0.18% on 1,106 — essentially flat on a large
verdict sample. The edge is a structural runline-underdog bias rather than a
box-score signal (no feature clears z = 1.2 over the price). 4/6 robustness.

### `pitcher_strikeouts` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +4.35% on 624; verdict +0.21% on 334 (underpowered). 6/6 robustness and
a strong signal — the opposing lineup's strikeout rate at z = 5.85, plus the
opposing bullpen at z = 3.85. Of the seven restricted markets this is the one
most likely to become a PASS once more priced sample exists.

### `h2h` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS **+0.35% on 966** — the thinnest pass in the set, barely above zero;
verdict −0.70% on 387; only 3/6 robustness. No feature in the harness beats the
moneyline price (best z = 1.89). What lifts it is the exact run-difference
distribution and the cross-market price identity (§7), not knowledge the book
lacks. Treat as unproven.

### `batter_runs_scored` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +1.26% on 5,049; verdict −1.74% on 1,623. 6/6 robustness on the full-OOS
gate, but the untouched half is negative on a large sample, which is the least
ambiguous "not yet" in the table.

### `pitcher_outs` — PASS_WITH_RESTRICTIONS
Full OOS +4.21% on 960; verdict +0.69% on 306 (underpowered). 4/6 robustness.
This market went from −1.03% to +4.21% purely on the starter-workload features
(§7), and it is the clearest demonstration in the project that the modelling
work, not the filter, moved the numbers.

### `batter_home_runs` — VETO
Full OOS −25.49% on 23 picks; verdict −44.16% on 14. There is real signal (the
Poisson HR model at the posted line, z = 6.19) and **no bettable board**. The
model likes 9,835 candidates; 53% of those are unders, and the shipped D-164
heavy-juice rule removes **99.5%** of them at a median price of −600. What
survives the veto and the EV floor is 23 bets in two and a half seasons. Dropping
the veto does not rescue it: the whole positive-EV under board is −0.80% on 5,203
picks and every over board loses double digits.
*To revisit:* Statcast contact quality — barrel rate, exit velocity, launch angle
— which this harness has no access to. Nothing in the box-score data fixes it.

## 5. Robustness — change one thing and look again

The whole pipeline was re-run under each variation, filter re-chosen from scratch
each time (`reports/robustness.csv`):

| Variant | Gate (full OOS) | Gate (verdict window) | Units (full OOS) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **locked (the headline)** | **10 / 11** | **3 / 11** | +793 |
| placebo lag 1 day | 7 / 11 | 6 / 11 | +705 |
| placebo lag 3 days | 9 / 11 | 5 / 11 | +855 |
| placebo lag 7 days | 8 / 11 | 4 / 11 | +669 |
| median book, no line shopping | 7 / 11 | 3 / 11 | +940 |
| warm-up 15%, 12 refit blocks | 9 / 11 | 7 / 11 | +2,542 |

**The placebo lag is the leakage test.** It forces the freshest box score a pick
may see to be a day, three days or a week older. A genuine form signal decays
slowly; a same-game leak dies at the first day of lag. Nothing collapses.

**Units are not comparable across rows.** Each variant re-chooses its own filter,
and a looser filter means far more picks at lower ROI — which is why the
refit-schedule row shows +2,542 units and the headline +793. **Compare the pass
counts, not the units.**

**Six markets clear the gate in every variant:** `batter_hits`, `batter_rbis`,
`batter_total_bases`, `batter_runs_scored`, `batter_strikeouts`,
`pitcher_strikeouts`. `spreads` and `pitcher_outs` clear 4/6, `h2h` 3/6, `totals`
2/6, `batter_home_runs` 1/6.

## 6. Calibration — are the probabilities any good?

A board is a comparison between a probability and a price, so being right about
direction is not enough. `calibration.py` scores the walk-forward probabilities
(`reports/calibration.csv`; reliability by decile in
`reports/calibration_reliability.csv`):

- **log loss and Brier score** per member and for the shipped ensemble;
- **slope and intercept** of the logistic recalibration — 1.0 and 0.0 is perfect,
  a slope below 1 means the model is over-confident;
- `price`, the market's own de-vigged number recalibrated against itself, is the
  bar: a member that cannot beat it knows less than the book does.

**The ensemble beats the price on log loss in 5 of 11 markets** — `batter_hits`,
`batter_rbis`, `batter_total_bases`, `batter_home_runs`, `batter_strikeouts`. All
three PASS markets are in that list. In the game and pitcher markets the price is
the better probability, which is consistent with §4: those boards earn what they
earn from price recalibration and structure, not from out-predicting the book.

## 7. What was built, and what each piece was worth

Three generations of feature work. Each was measured the same way: `attribute.py`
scores **both feature sets through one identical filter and side policy**, so a
feature gain can never be confused with a filter gain (`run_final.py` re-chooses
its filter every run, so a naive before/after would confound the two).

### Generation 1 → 2: park, bullpen, starter workload
(`reports/attribution_pass2.csv`)

| Feature family | What it is | Why it was missing |
|---|---|---|
| **park environment** | runs and home runs already hit in **this** ballpark, last 100 games there, shrunk to the league rate | recorded as "needs park data"; a park factor needs no new source |
| **bullpen** | the relief corps' as-of runs per out, K and BB per batter faced, innings per game | recorded as blocked on `cache_mlb_historical_bullpen`, which `harness_readonly` cannot read — but every relief appearance is also a box-score row |
| **starter workload** | pitch budget, pitches per out, walk rate, five-start form, and the start-to-start spread of his own outs | `pitches_thrown` was in the dump, populated on every pitcher row, and nothing read it |

Under an identical filter: **6/11 → 9/11** clearing the full-OOS gate. The three
that flipped were `pitcher_outs` (+4.49pp), `totals` (+3.89pp) and
`pitcher_strikeouts` (+2.92pp) — exactly the three those features were built for.

### Generation 2 → 3: exact run distribution, Elo, cross-market identity, opportunity
(`reports/attribution_pass3.csv`)

| Addition | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Skellam run difference** | the difference of two Poisson team scores, evaluated exactly at the posted number, instead of a normal approximation with a fixed 4.3-run standard deviation. Ties are renormalised away, because baseball has none |
| **Elo** | a running team rating with margin-of-victory damping and season reversion — causal by construction, since it only ever moves forward |
| **Pythagorean** | runs scored and allowed turned into an expected win rate |
| **cross-market price identity** | the total fixes the sum of the two team lambdas and the moneyline fixes their difference, so the book's own three numbers imply each other. Where they disagree, the disagreement is visible at bet time and needs no model |
| **opportunity model** | expected plate appearances from the lineup slot, the team's PA per game and the player's start share, instead of the player's own recent appearances; plus a binomial view of the rare-event markets, since a batter cannot hit two home runs in one at-bat |

Under an identical filter: **9/11 → 10/11**. The market that flipped was `h2h`
(−2.91% → +0.35%), with `totals` +3.48pp, `pitcher_strikeouts` +2.62pp and
`batter_rbis` +1.91pp also improving.

**An honest note on generation 3.** It buys one more market on the full-OOS gate
and costs one on the verdict window (generation 2 read 9 / 4; generation 3 reads
10 / 3), because `pitcher_strikeouts` and `batter_runs_scored` moved by 1–3
points on 300–1,600 verdict picks, which is inside the noise. Generation 3 is the
shipped one because its gain was measured under an identical filter and because
it averages **more** verdict-window passes across the six robustness variants
(4.7 against 3.8) — not because it had the better headline.

### One bug worth recording
The as-of roll-ups initially returned almost nothing: pandas 3 parses a date
string to `datetime64[us]`, and the day counter divided the int64 view by
nanoseconds-per-day, collapsing every date into two buckets. The direction of
that error is worth noting — it **suppressed** signal rather than inventing it.
Fixed in `frames.to_day`, which now casts to `datetime64[D]`.

## 8. Point-in-time integrity

Every number here depends on one property: a feature attached to a game may only
read box scores that finished on an **earlier calendar date**.

- **`check_asof.py`** recomputes 200 sampled rows from each of the three newest
  feature tables with a naive filter that cannot be subtly wrong — *take every
  earlier row, average it* — and asserts the fast roll-up agrees. It also asserts
  the stronger thing: that the value **would change** if the game's own day were
  let in. **600 rows, 0 mismatches**, 562 of them live tests of the cut.
- **Placebo lag** (§5) asks the same question from the other end.
- **Fold boundaries are timestamps, not row indices**, so two picks on the same
  game never straddle a training cut.
- **Prices are the entry snapshot**, roughly 5.6 hours before first pitch — the
  shipped convention (`best_price.ts` `selectBestSameLineBook`). No closing line
  is used anywhere.
- **The date key** is the warehouse's own `game_date::text`. For late starts this
  can be the UTC date rather than the local one, which groups about 22% of games
  with the following day. The direction matters: a UTC shift and a postponement
  both move a game's label **later**, never earlier, so the effect is to hide
  some history from a feature, never to leak a result into one. 804 rows (0.9%)
  are postponements, where the priced snapshot is older than the game as played.

## 9. Production eligibility

| Category | Markets | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully validated** | `batter_hits`, `batter_total_bases`, `batter_strikeouts` | Eligible to enable at normal stake, with the side policy in the matrix. Monitor realised ROI against the backtest. |
| **Restricted** | `batter_rbis`, `totals`, `spreads`, `pitcher_strikeouts`, `h2h`, `batter_runs_scored`, `pitcher_outs` | Paper-trade or reduced stake with a documented stop. `pitcher_strikeouts` and `pitcher_outs` are the two most likely to graduate once more priced sample exists; `h2h` and `totals` are the two least trustworthy. |
| **Vetoed** | `batter_home_runs` | Do not enable. It needs Statcast contact quality **and** a relaxed juice rule, and the second without the first would be worse than doing nothing. |

**None of this is a promise about the future.** A backtest measures what a rule
would have earned on a sample that has already happened. Books get sharper, lines
move, and a market that cleared a gate in 2024–26 can stop clearing it.

## 10. What this does NOT claim

- It is **not** a live result. Nothing here has been deployed, and the live
  production pick stream is a harder population than the warehouse era.
- It does **not** re-fit the production scorer. The shipped scorer's disagreement
  with the price carries no information in any market; the filter sits *after*
  scoring.
- Board ROI assumes the best number at that line was available and taken, which
  is the shipped convention. The no-line-shopping floor is the median-book row in
  §5: eight markets instead of ten.
- "Ten markets pass" refers to the **full-OOS gate only**. The production count
  is **three**.
- The per-market lever search (`reports/improve_*.csv`) was run against the
  generation-2 feature set and was not re-run after generation 3. It is kept as
  an experiment record, not as a result.
